Methodology How source rows become MacroTrace observations

One row, one observation. Every row is a series ID, date, value, unit, frequency, source observation. Dates remain at their publisher’s native daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual frequency.

Cleaning. Header rows, blanks, non-numeric values, duplicate dates, missing FRED values, and null market closes are omitted. No observations are fabricated between releases.

Comparability. Positive quantities and indexes use (last ÷ boundary − 1) × 100; rates use (last − boundary) × 100 basis points; signed indexes use point differences. Calendar horizons use an exact boundary observation when present, otherwise the prior observed value. Unmeasurable short windows stay unavailable.

Cycles and ranks. Macro cycles use native frequency levels or 12-month percentage growth. Cycle and momentum paths use full-history midpoint percentile ranks for ties, preserving extremes on a common 0–100 scale. The change momentum score compares the latest change with its historical mean in sample-standard-deviation units. Breadth is the share of measured series moving higher; acceleration compares each change with its preceding complete-period change, not with other units.

Returns and risk. Drawdown is 100 × (value ÷ running peak − 1). Volatility is sample standard deviation of native-frequency log returns multiplied by 100 × √periods per year, using 252, 52, 12, 4, or 1. Market windows of at least one year show CAGR as 100 × ((last ÷ boundary)^(1 / elapsed years) − 1); the median averages the two middle values when the count is even.

Rolling diagnostics. Annual change paths use trailing calendar years. Rolling volatility requires a full calendar-year boundary and enough adjacent native changes; macro variability is ranked within each source’s own history. Change distributions use the first selected series, native-period changes, and equal-width bins spanning every valid value—no outliers are removed. Positive and negative colors indicate sign, not economic desirability. Daily headlines are close-to-close; monthly, quarterly, and annual cards retain their actual periods. “Updated” is the data-check timestamp, not a trade timestamp.

Calendar comparisons. Correlation uses Pearson’s coefficient on aligned changes, never raw levels. The coarsest selected frequency determines month, quarter, or year comparisons; incomplete periods and gaps are excluded, and at least three paired changes are required. Heat maps use the last observation of each complete period and label percent, basis-point, or point units; color measures direction, not desirability.

Freshness and interpretation. The bundled snapshot is refreshed daily, not streamed live. An observation date is a reference date, not a publication date. Yahoo history may be in progress and depends on provider availability. Upstream failures retain the last successful snapshot and are disclosed in each entry’s details.

Research histories. Fama–French and Damodaran histories preserve source-file details, hashes, and transformation descriptions. Compounded research indexes use indexₜ = indexₜ₋₁ × (1 + returnₜ / 100) from 100; industry portfolios and factors are not investable funds. Annual histories remain annual, with no synthetic daily path. Shiller series are retrospective research reconstructions; commodity spot prices are not futures investment returns.

Explicit proxy extensions. Series labeled SIM use public research history before a documented anchor and observed ETF adjusted-close changes afterward. They are not actual pre-inception ETF returns. Monthly or annual period-end sampling excludes incomplete periods. Each entry identifies both sources, the join date, construction differences, and measured overlap; the original daily ticker remains separate.

Historical archives. Archived series retain their real end dates and historical definitions. Daily checks do not make discontinued data current, and incompatible series are never silently joined. Coverage includes a research index's initial reference baseline where applicable.
