Methodology How source rows become MacroTrace observations

One row, one observation. Every row is a series ID, date, value, unit, frequency, source observation. Dates remain at their publisher’s native daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual frequency.

Cleaning. Header rows, blanks, non-numeric values, duplicate dates, missing FRED values, and null market closes are omitted. No observations are fabricated between releases.

Comparability. Positive quantities and indexes use (last ÷ boundary − 1) × 100; rates use (last − boundary) × 100 basis points; signed indexes use point differences. Calendar horizons use an exact boundary observation when present, otherwise the prior observed value. Unmeasurable short windows stay unavailable.

Cycles and ranks. Macro cycles use native frequency levels or 12-month percentage growth, standardized as (value − historical mean) ÷ sample standard deviation. Percentiles use the same signal with midpoint ranks for ties; momentum compares the latest change with its own history. Breadth is the share of measurable selected series moving higher.

Returns and risk. Drawdown is 100 × (value ÷ running peak − 1). Volatility is sample standard deviation of native-frequency log returns multiplied by 100 × √periods per year, using 252, 52, 12, 4, or 1. Market windows of at least one year show CAGR as 100 × ((last ÷ boundary)^(1 / elapsed years) − 1); the median averages the two middle values when the count is even.

Calendar comparisons. Correlation uses Pearson’s coefficient on aligned changes, never raw levels. The coarsest selected frequency determines month, quarter, or year comparisons; incomplete periods and gaps are excluded, and at least three paired changes are required. Heat maps use the last observation of each complete period and label percent, basis-point, or point units; color measures direction, not desirability.

Freshness and interpretation. The bundled snapshot is refreshed daily, not streamed live. An observation date is a reference date, not a publication date. Yahoo history may be in progress and depends on provider availability. Upstream failures retain the last successful snapshot and are disclosed in each entry’s details.

Research histories. Fama–French and Damodaran histories preserve source-file details, hashes, and transformation descriptions. Compounded research indexes use indexₜ = indexₜ₋₁ × (1 + returnₜ / 100) from 100; industry portfolios and factors are not investable funds. Annual histories remain annual, with no synthetic daily path or undisclosed ETF splice.
