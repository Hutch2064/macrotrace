The readout

Eight findings, each tied to the underlying observations.

This report is the narrative half of MacroTrace. It uses eight reproducible comparisons to describe the current U.S. macro backdrop; the dashboard is the exploratory half, with separate Macro and Markets modes so economic levels are never confused with security returns.

Methodology

One row is one observed value on one date.

Sources and rows. Macroeconomic series come from FRED; optional market histories use Yahoo Finance adjusted close. Long-history research indexes use Fama–French monthly returns and Damodaran annual returns with source-file hashes. One row is one series_id, date, value, unit, frequency, source observation, and every series retains its source link.

Cleaning. Header rows, blank or “.” FRED observations, non-numeric values, duplicate dates, and Yahoo rows with null adjusted closes are dropped. Dates remain at their native daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual frequency; no observations are fabricated between releases.

Comparability. Positive quantities and indexes use (last ÷ boundary − 1) × 100. Rates use (last − boundary) × 100 basis points; signed indexes use point differences. Calendar horizons use an exact boundary observation when present, otherwise the prior observed value. Short windows that cannot be measured at the native frequency remain unavailable. Indexed market paths start at 100; their source may be a price, adjusted close, or research index, as disclosed in the table.

Cycles and ranks. Macro cycle charts use 12-month percentage growth for positive quantities, and levels for rates or signed indexes. Each is standardized as (value − historical mean) ÷ sample standard deviation. Percentiles use the same economic signal and midpoint ranks for ties, not the raw CPI price level. Momentum scores compare the latest horizon change with its own historical mean and standard deviation. Breadth is the share of measurable selected series moving higher—not a claim that the economy is improving.

Returns and risk. Drawdown is 100 × (value ÷ running peak − 1) within the selected window, for positive market paths only; moves between native observations are not captured. Volatility is the sample standard deviation of native-frequency log returns multiplied by 100 × √periods per year, using 252, 52, 12, 4, or 1. Missing observations are not treated as single-period returns. Market windows of at least one year show compound annual growth as 100 × ((last ÷ boundary)^(1 / elapsed years) − 1). The median averages the two middle values when the count is even.

Calendar comparisons. Correlation uses Pearson’s coefficient on aligned changes, never raw price levels. The coarsest selected frequency determines month, quarter, or year comparisons; incomplete aggregated periods and gaps are excluded. At least three paired changes are required, with sample counts shown. Heat maps use the last observed level of each complete period; each row states percent, basis-point, or point units. Color measures direction, not economic desirability.

Freshness and interpretation. The public snapshot is refreshed daily, not streamed live. The ticker banner shows observation dates; these are not publication dates. A monthly observation may be dated at the start of its reference month. Yahoo search/history is cached, its latest daily bar may be in progress, and availability depends on the provider. The table exposes source, frequency, full coverage, and the latest successful check.

Research histories. Fama–French and Damodaran returns are compounded as indexₜ = indexₜ₋₁ × (1 + returnₜ / 100) from 100. Industry portfolios and long-short size/value factors are academic research series, not investable funds. Gold is a price-return history. Annual histories remain annual; no synthetic daily path or undisclosed ETF splice is presented. Exact source hashes and transformation descriptions travel with the snapshot.
